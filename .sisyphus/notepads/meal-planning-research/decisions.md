## Strategic Positioning Decisions for Fenne

### Research Date: February 12, 2026

---

## CORE POSITIONING DECISION

**Fenne is a PERSONAL RECIPE ORGANIZER, not a recipe database.**

This is the single most important positioning decision that will guide all marketing copy, feature descriptions, and user communication.

---

## POSITIONING FRAMEWORK

### 1. Primary Value Proposition
**"Your personal recipe collection, beautifully organized"**

Alternative options:
- "Organize your recipes. Plan your meals. Shop smarter."
- "All your recipes in one place, synced everywhere"
- "Recipe chaos solved. Meal planning simplified."

### 2. Target User
**Recipe collectors with scattered collections**

Characteristics:
- Already has recipes they love (not looking for new recipes)
- Recipes scattered across: bookmarks, screenshots, Instagram saves, Pinterest, recipe cards
- Wants control over their meal planning
- Values organization and accessibility
- Likely cooks regularly (not beginners looking for guidance)

### 3. Core Problem We Solve
**"Recipe chaos"** - the pain of scattered, disorganized recipes

Problem statement options:
- "Recipes scattered across bookmarks, screenshots, and recipe cards?"
- "Tired of losing track of recipes you love?"
- "That recipe you saw last week... where did you save it?"

## DIFFERENTIATION STRATEGY

### vs. Paprika (Main Competitor)
**Fenne's Advantages:**
- Modern, beautiful interface (Paprika feels dated)
- Better social media import (Instagram/TikTok native)
- Single subscription for all platforms (Paprika charges per OS)
- More intuitive organization

**Messaging:**
- "Paprika, but modern"
- "The recipe organizer for 2026"
- "Beautiful design meets powerful organization"

### vs. Plan to Eat
**Fenne's Advantages:**
- Cleaner UX
- Better mobile experience
- More visual/photo-focused
- Simpler onboarding

**Messaging:**
- "Simpler, more beautiful meal planning"
- "Organization that doesn't feel like work"

### vs. Mealime/Recipe Database Apps
**Fenne's Advantages:**
- User's own recipes (not pre-populated)
- Full control over collection
- No recipe discovery noise

**Messaging:**
- "Your recipes, not ours"
- "Organize what you already love"
- "No recipe browsing, just your favorites"

## SYNC FEATURE COMMUNICATION

### Primary Messaging:
**"Access your recipes on any device"**

### Use Case Scenarios (for marketing copy):
1. "Plan on your laptop, cook from your tablet, shop with your phone"
2. "Start a recipe at home, finish shopping at the store"
3. "Add recipes anywhere, access them everywhere"

### Technical Details (for FAQ only):
- Account-based sync
- Automatic cloud backup
- Real-time updates
- Works across iOS, Android, Web

### What NOT to say:
- Avoid technical jargon ("cloud synchronization protocol")
- Don't lead with sync as a feature (it's expected)
- Don't emphasize "cloud" (users don't care about the tech)

---

## SHOPPING FEATURE COMMUNICATION

### Primary Messaging:
**"From meal plan to grocery list in one tap"**

### Key Benefits to Emphasize:
1. **Automation:** "One tap" generation
2. **Organization:** "Sorted by aisle"
3. **Intelligence:** "Duplicates combined"
4. **Time savings:** "Faster shopping"
5. **Completeness:** "Never forget an ingredient"

### User Flow to Highlight:
1. Plan your week (drag recipes to calendar)
2. Tap "Generate List" (one button)
3. Shop with organized list (aisle-sorted)
4. Check off as you go (real-time sync)

### What NOT to say:
- Avoid "AI-powered" (feels like overkill)
- Don't emphasize technical features (parsing, NLP)
- Don't promise delivery integration (unless we have it)

## ONBOARDING STRATEGY

### First-Time User Experience:

**Option 1: Empty State (Recommended)**
- Show empty recipe collection
- Clear CTA: "Add your first recipe"
- Multiple import options visible
- Quick tutorial on import methods

**Option 2: Starter Recipes (Alternative)**
- Show 3-5 example recipes
- Clear messaging: "These are examples to help you get started"
- Easy way to delete/hide examples
- Prominent "Add your own recipes" CTA

**Recommendation:** Option 1 (Empty State)
- Reinforces "your collection" positioning
- Avoids confusion about whose recipes they are
- Cleaner, more focused experience

### Import Tutorial:
Show users how to save from:
1. Websites (paste URL)
2. Instagram/TikTok (share extension)
3. Photos (cookbook/recipe card)
4. Manual entry (write your own)

## DECISION CHECKLIST

Before publishing any marketing copy, ask:

1. ✅ Does it emphasize "YOUR recipes" (not ours)?
2. ✅ Does it focus on organization (not discovery)?
3. ✅ Does it mention source flexibility (save from anywhere)?
4. ✅ Does it emphasize user control (organize your way)?
5. ✅ Does it avoid "recipe database" language?
6. ✅ Does it solve "recipe chaos" problem?
7. ✅ Does it speak to recipe collectors (not beginners)?
8. ✅ Does it position sync as access (not tech)?

If any answer is NO, revise the copy.
